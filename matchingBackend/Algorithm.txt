# Matching Algorithm – Scoring Breakdown

The matching engine ranks potential farmer–buyer matches using six main
factors worth a maximum of **100 base points**.

A buyer with recurring demand may receive an additional **5-point bonus**.

The final match score is always capped at **100%**.

---

## Factors and Weights

| Factor | Weight | Description |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| **Product Match** | 30 | Exact match = 30, partial match = 20, no match = 0. |
| **Quantity Feasibility** | 20 | Buyer demand <= seller supply = 20; ≤1.5x supply = 15; ≤2.5x = 8; >2.5x = 2. |
| **Price Alignment** | 20 | Seller price ≤ buyer budget = 20; ≤1.2x budget = 14; ≤1.5x = 7; >1.5x = 0. |
| **Distance Score** | 15 | ≤50 km = 15; ≤150 km = 12; ≤300 km = 8; ≤500 km = 4; >500 km = 1. |
| **Transport Cost** | 10 | ≤N$100 = 10; ≤N$300 = 7; ≤N$600 = 4; ≤N$1,000 = 2; >N$1,000 = 0. |
| **Availability** | 5 | Active and currently available listings receive 5 points. |

Maximum base score:

`30 + 20 + 20 + 15 + 10 + 5 = 100`

---

## Recurring Demand Bonus

If the buyer needs the product on a recurring basis, the matching engine
can add an additional **5 points**.

For example:

- `once` = no bonus
- `weekly` = recurring-demand bonus
- `monthly` = recurring-demand bonus

Because the final score is capped at 100, a match can never exceed
**100%**.

Example:

`Base Score = 97`

`Recurring Bonus = 5`

`97 + 5 = 102`

Final displayed score:

`100%`

---

## Distance Calculation

The matching engine uses the **Haversine formula** to estimate the
great-circle distance between the buyer and seller using latitude and
longitude coordinates for supported Namibian towns.

This allows the system to favor nearby suppliers when all other factors
are similar.

If a location cannot be identified by the matching engine, it should not
receive the same distance advantage as a known nearby location.

---

## Transport Cost Estimation

Estimated transport cost is calculated using:

`cost = distance (km) × N$2.50 × weight in tonnes`

The quantity is converted into tonnes before calculating the estimated
transport cost.

A minimum transport charge of:

`N$50`

is applied.

The estimated transport cost is capped at:

`N$20,000`

This value is then used as one of the factors in the match score.

---

## Price Difference

Price difference is calculated as:

`price_difference = seller_price - buyer_budget`

### Negative value

A negative result means the farmer's price is **below the buyer's maximum
budget**.

Example:

`Seller price = N$25/kg`

`Buyer budget = N$30/kg`

`25 - 30 = -5`

The seller is therefore:

`N$5/kg below budget`

This is generally a strong price match.

### Zero

A result of zero means:

`Seller price = Buyer budget`

The seller is exactly within the buyer's maximum price.

### Positive value

A positive result means the seller's asking price is **above the buyer's
budget**.

Example:

`Seller price = N$35/kg`

`Buyer budget = N$30/kg`

`35 - 30 = 5`

The seller is:

`N$5/kg above budget`

This match may still appear, but it receives a lower price-alignment
score.

---

## Example Match

A buyer in Windhoek requests:

- Product: Tomatoes
- Quantity: 100 kg
- Maximum price: N$30/kg
- Frequency: Once

A farmer in Windhoek lists:

- Product: Tomatoes
- Quantity available: 120 kg
- Price: N$25/kg
- Available now

The matching engine evaluates:

1. Product compatibility
2. Whether the farmer has enough quantity
3. Whether the farmer's price fits the buyer's budget
4. Distance between buyer and farmer
5. Estimated transport cost
6. Product availability

The resulting score determines where that farmer appears in the buyer's
ranked match results.

Higher-scoring matches are shown before lower-scoring matches.
